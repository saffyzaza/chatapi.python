# ULTRA DETAILED: Research - Factors of Raw Fish Consumption (Non Koon, Sisaket)

## Research Overview
- **Title:** Factors affecting consumption of raw fish among people in Lao Kwang Sub-district, Non Koon District, Sisaket Province.
- **Published:** 2022 (Ubon Ratchathani Rajabhat University).
- **Study Area:** Lao Kwang Sub-district, Non Koon District, Sisaket.
- **Sample Size:** 126 people (Aged 15+).

## Key Findings
- **Prevalence:** 65.90% of the subjects consume food prepared from raw fish.
- **Popular Dishes:** 
    1. Som Tam with raw Pla-ra (29.92%)
    2. Koi Pla (21.69%)
    3. Jeaw Bong with raw Pla-ra (19.28%)
- **Statistically Significant Factors (OR - Odds Ratio):**
    - **Occupation:** Farmers are **21.86 times** more likely to consume raw fish compared to unemployed/other groups.
    - **Education:** Those with only primary education are **15.68 times** more likely than those with a bachelor's degree.
    - **Family Habits:** Family consumption increases the likelihood by **9.52 times**.
    - **Attitude:** A negative or neutral attitude towards prevention increases risk by **12.11 times**.

## Rationale for Consumption
- **Taste:** 27.71% believe raw fish tastes better than cooked fish.
- **Frequency:** 26.51% believe infrequent consumption is safe.
- **Culture:** 19.28% view it as a long-standing cultural tradition.
- **Convenience:** 15.66% find it easier to prepare.

## Health Implications
- **Liver Fluke (Opisthorchis viverrini):** The primary cause of infection from raw freshwater fish.
- **Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA):** Reinfection or chronic infection significantly increases the risk of bile duct cancer.
- **Prevalence in Sisaket (2019 data):** Highest liver fluke prevalence was in Wang Hin (26.76%), Prang Ku (25.27%), and Sri Rattana (20.90%).

## Recommendations
- **Behavioral Change:** Public health officers must focus on changing attitudes towards raw Pla-ra.
- **Social Marketing:** Use community events and religious festivals (e.g., Bun Khao Chi) to promote cooked fish consumption.
- **Target Groups:** Focus on males and agricultural workers who are high-risk.
